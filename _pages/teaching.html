--- layout: single title: "Teaching & Mentorship" permalink: /teaching/ author_profile: false --- My approach to teaching emphasizes active inquiry, experiential learning, and mentorship. I aim to cultivate curiosity about the natural world while helping students develop the skills and confidence to investigate scientific questions independently. ## Inquiry-based learning I believe students learn science most effectively when they participate in the process of discovery. Rather than focusing solely on the transmission of information, I encourage students to make observations, develop hypotheses, evaluate evidence, and interpret results. This approach helps students understand not only scientific concepts, but also how scientific knowledge is developed and tested. ## Experiential and field-based learning Field experiences provide opportunities to connect concepts discussed in the classroom with processes observed in nature. As teaching faculty with the Juneau Icefield Research Program in 2026, I developed lessons on primary succession in deglaciated environments and island biogeography on nunataks. Students used plant and insect surveys, field sampling, and data interpretation to investigate ecological questions in environments undergoing change. ## Collections-based and laboratory teaching I use specimens and research collections to make biological and geological concepts tangible. During teaching appointments at the University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign, I developed laboratory activities incorporating paleobotanical, herbarium, and entomological collections. These activities gave students opportunities to examine biological diversity, compare structures, and connect observations with broader questions in ecology and evolution. ## Quantitative reasoning and collaborative problem-solving I incorporate data analysis and collaborative activities to help students evaluate competing explanations and communicate scientific findings. Field exercises investigating tallgrass prairie biodiversity and ecological processes allowed students to work with observations and data. I also use student feedback to refine activities and support different approaches to learning. ## Student-centered mentorship and independence My mentorship philosophy is grounded in the belief that students, particularly graduate students, should have the freedom to explore research questions they are passionate about. As a mentor, I provide guidance, constructive feedback, and practical support while encouraging students to take ownership of their ideas and develop as independent researchers. I have mentored twelve undergraduate researchers and one graduate student in paleobiology, statistical modeling, and machine learning. Three undergraduate mentees completed senior thesis projects and presented their findings through posters or oral presentations. ## Teaching experience and recognition Across thirteen semesters of teaching appointments at Hofstra University and the University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign, I have supported classroom, laboratory, and online instruction. Courses have included Honors Organismal Biology, Ecology, Honors Ecology and Evolution, The Biology of Dinosaurs, and introductory biology. My teaching development includes a minor in College Education, teaching certificates through the University of Illinois Center for Innovation in Teaching & Learning, and mentorship training through CIMER. I was recognized on the University of Illinois Teachers Ranked as Excellent list from 2020 through 2023.
