TEACHING & MENTORSHIP

Learning Through Discovery

Teaching is grounded in active inquiry, experiential learning, and direct engagement with scientific evidence. Fieldwork, scientific collections, observation, and creative exercises connect biological and geological concepts with the natural world while fostering curiosity, critical thinking, and scientific independence.

Teaching Methods

01

Collections-Based Learning

Fossil, herbarium, and entomological collections provide direct evidence for investigating morphology, biodiversity, evolutionary relationships, and ancient ecosystems. Specimen examination connects theoretical concepts with tangible scientific material.

02

Field-Based Learning

Field observations, ecological surveys, and hypothesis-driven investigations connect classroom concepts with natural environments. These approaches emphasize biodiversity, ecological relationships, and the influence of environmental change.

03

Inquiry & Active Learning

Observation, hypothesis testing, scientific illustration, and collaborative problem-solving encourage critical thinking and evidence-based reasoning. Creative exercises provide opportunities to investigate scientific questions from different perspectives.

04

Hands-On Laboratory Learning

Specimen preparation, comparative morphology, microscopy, and botanical identification connect field observations with laboratory investigations. These techniques emphasize careful documentation, scientific interpretation, and practical research skills.

Teaching in the Field and Laboratory

Mentorship

Mentorship is one of the most rewarding aspects of teaching and research, offering opportunities to foster curiosity, build confidence, and support students in discovering their scientific interests. Central to this approach is the belief that students, particularly graduate researchers, should have the freedom to pursue questions they are passionate about while receiving individualized guidance, encouragement, and constructive feedback. Creating a supportive and collaborative environment allows students to develop their ideas, overcome challenges, and grow as independent researchers.
