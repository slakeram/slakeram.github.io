TEACHING & MENTORSHIP

Learning Through Discovery

My teaching philosophy incorporates active inquiry, experiential learning, and direct engagement with scientific evidence. Through fieldwork, scientific collections, and hands-on activities, students connect biological and geological concepts with the natural world. These experiences encourage curiosity, strengthen critical thinking, and develop the confidence to investigate scientific questions independently.

Teaching Methods

01

Collections-Based Learning

Fossil, herbarium, and entomological collections provide opportunities to investigate morphology, biodiversity, and evolutionary relationships. Examining specimens firsthand allows students to connect theoretical concepts with tangible scientific evidence and develop skills in observation and interpretation.

02

Interdisciplinary Approaches in the Field

Fieldwork provides opportunities to explore the connections between geology, biology, and ecology. By examining landforms, soils, vegetation, and ecological processes together, students gain a broader understanding of how physical and biological systems interact to shape landscapes and ecosystems over time.

03

Inquiry-Based & Hands-On Learning

Observation, hypothesis testing, microscopy, scientific illustration, and collaborative problem-solving encourage students to investigate scientific questions directly. These activities develop critical thinking, careful documentation, and the ability to interpret evidence independently.

04

Developing Scientific Perspectives

Scientific understanding develops through observation, discussion, and the interpretation of evidence. Encouraging students to consider different explanations, challenge assumptions, and revisit their conclusions helps them develop a deeper understanding of the natural world and the confidence to think independently.

Teaching in the Field and Laboratory

Mentorship

Mentorship is one of the most rewarding aspects of teaching and research, providing opportunities to foster curiosity and help students develop their scientific interests. I believe students, particularly graduate researchers, should have the freedom to pursue questions they are passionate about while receiving guidance and support tailored to their individual learning styles. By fostering a collaborative and supportive environment, I aim to help students develop confidence in their ideas, navigate challenges, and grow as independent researchers.
